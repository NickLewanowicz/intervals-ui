# Modern marathon/half-marathon fueling — evidence and coach guidance (2025-2026)

**Purpose.** Extend the coach (knowledge base: `packages/core/src/prompts/knowledge.ts`, "Fueling"
bullet) with current, freely-citable sports-nutrition guidance, especially what changed in the
~6-12 months around the fall of the two-hour marathon barrier in a record-eligible race.

**Boundary up front:** *Individualize everything, practice everything in training, and nothing here
is medical advice.* Athletes with GI disorders, diabetes, disordered-eating history, kidney issues,
or on medication (diuretics, SSRIs, stimulants) should involve a physician/registered dietitian.

---

## 1. Context: the sub-2 era and what it revealed about fueling

- **The barrier fell in a sanctioned race.** Sabastian Sawe won the **2026 London Marathon in
  1:59:30** (April 26, 2026) — the first official, record-eligible sub-2:00 — with Yomif Kejelcha
  also under two (1:59:41) in the same race. Context and history:
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-hour_marathon (Kipchoge's 1:59:40 INEOS 1:59 Challenge, 2019,
  was an exhibition with rotating pacers; Kelvin Kiptum's official WR 2:00:35, Chicago 2023, made
  sub-2 seem imminent).
- **Fueling was a headline of the sub-2 story.** Sawe averaged **~115 g carbohydrate/hour**,
  built up from a ~60 g/h baseline through structured gut training and lab testing, built on
  Maurten hydrogel products; Kejelcha took in only **~70 g/h** and also ran under two — elite
  fueling is individualized, not one number:
  - Runner's World on Sawe's fueling: https://www.runnersworld.com/nutrition-weight-loss/a71178309/sabastian-sawe-sub-2-fueling-tips
  - The Guardian, inside the Maurten lab behind the plan: https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2026/may/16/sabastian-sawe-secret-sauce-inside-lab-sub-two-hour-marathon-maurten-sweden
  - Marathon Handbook analysis: https://marathonhandbook.com/how-sabastian-sawe-fueled-his-sub-two-hour-marathon-london
  - Kejelcha contrast (~70 g/h): https://nduranz.com/blogs/blogs/how-to-fuel-for-a-2-hour-marathon-lessons-from-london
- **Best-documented elite baseline:** Eliud Kipchoge took ~**100 g/h** (Maurten Drink Mix/gels,
  bottle handoffs roughly every 20 min) during the INEOS 1:59 build.
- **Skepticism is healthy:** most record-race fueling numbers are not independently verified —
  Science of Sport (Ross Tucker) publicly criticized coverage that assumed ~100 g/h for Ruth
  Chepngetich's women's WR 2:09:56 (Chicago 2024) with zero published intake data. Cite elite
  fueling as reported practice, not proven causation. (https://www.sportsscientists.com/ — coverage
  aggregated there.)

## 2. Carbohydrate-per-hour: the current guidance landscape

**Primary public review:** Cao, W. et al. (2025). *A review of carbohydrate supplementation
approaches…* (open access): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11901785/

| Event duration | In-race carbohydrate (Cao 2025) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| ≤60 min | mouth-rinse optional; no intake required | fed states vs fasted matter little at this length |
| 60-150 min (fast half marathons, ~90-150 min) | **30-60 g/h** | newer practitioner guidance allows 60-90 g/h for fast, gut-trained half runners: https://lauranorrisrunning.substack.com/p/the-new-guidelines-of-how-to-fuel |
| >150 min (marathon) | **60-90 g/h** | established consensus range |
| >90 g/h | historically little added benefit; now feasible for *trained* athletes | see below |

- **The 90 → 120 g/h shift.** Recent perspective work (Morton et al., *From Metabolism to Medals*,
  2026) argues trained athletes can push intake from the classic 90 g/h ceiling toward **120 g/h**
  using multiple transportable carbohydrates: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S002231662600091X
  This matches reported elite practice (Sawe 115 g/h). Cao 2025 still cautions that >90 g/h adds
  little for most athletes *unless glycogen is running low* — i.e., high rates are a tool for
  long, hard efforts, not a status symbol.
- **Supporting trial evidence:** in a trail-marathon RCT, **120 g/h vs 60 g/h** limited
  neuromuscular fatigue and improved recovery (without clearly beating 90 g/h on performance):
  https://www.researchgate.net/publication/342956265
- **Practical middle ground (vendor but data-aligned):** most athletes land at **30-90 g/h**
  depending on duration, intensity, and gut training:
  https://www.precisionhydration.com/performance-advice/nutrition/how-much-carbohydrate-carbs-athletes-per-hour/

### Multiple transportable carbohydrates (Carb-1 / Carb-2 physiology)

- Intestinal absorption is transporter-limited: **glucose via SGLT1** (~1.0-1.2 g/min oxidation
  ceiling alone), **fructose via GLUT5**. Combining sources raises total oxidation to
  **~1.5 g/min (~90 g/h)** — the physiological basis for 2:1 glucose:fructose blends
  (Cao 2025, §3; classic work by Jeukendrup).
- Ratios near **2:1 glucose:fructose** are the practical standard (recent trials explore ~1:0.8
  glucose:fructose for GI comfort); drink concentrations in practice range from 6-10% solutions to
  elite concentrated gels ~200 g/L. Cao 2025 covers both.
- Practical mapping: 1 typical gel ≈ 22-25 g carb; 500 ml of a 6-8% sports drink ≈ 30-40 g.
  60-90 g/h therefore = "1 gel + strong mix per 20-30 min," fed steadily rather than in big doses.

## 3. Gut training (tolerance is trainable)

- **Systematic review:** Martinez et al. (2023), *Sports Medicine* — repeated gut-challenge
  protocols (2 weeks) reduced GI discomfort by **~47%** and improved symptom scores during
  running: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-023-01841-0
- **Jeukendrup's public protocol** (GSSI, *Training the Gut for Athletes*): train with high-carb
  intake in targeted sessions (daily or ~weekly), expect adaptation over **6-10 weeks**:
  https://www.gssiweb.org/sports-science-exchange/article/training-the-gut-for-athletes
- **Concrete running protocol (via Cao 2025):** 10 sessions over 2 weeks — 60 min easy running
  with a **30 g gel at 0, 20 and 40 min** — improved tolerance and performance.
- **Coach-facing build rule:** raise race-pace fueling by **~5-10 g/h per week** toward the target,
  practicing in the long run and (later) in quality sessions. Higher-carb daily diet supports
  transporter downregulation reversal (GLUT5/SGLT1 expression responds to habitual intake).

## 4. Onset timing, race morning, caffeine

- **Start early.** Blood glucose rises within ~15 min of intake; feed from the **first 15-25 min**
  of the race and then **every 20-30 min**, rather than waiting for hunger/depletion (Cao 2025).
  Late fueling cannot catch up an early deficit in a marathon.
- **Avoid a large carb dose in the 30-60 min before the start** (reactive-hypoglycemia risk; Cao
  2025). Race-morning structure:
  - **Carb load:** 8-12 g/kg/day for 24-48 h before a >90-min event (ISSN position stand,
    Kerksick et al. 2017: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5596471/); Cao 2025 suggests
    10-12 g/kg/day from 36-48 h out for marathons.
  - **Pre-race meal:** ~1-4 g/kg carbohydrate, 1-4 h before the start (Kerksick 2017); Cao's
    conservative elite variant: ≤75 g carbs at least 2 h before, optionally a low-GI drink after.
    For a 70 kg runner: ~70-280 g carbs at breakfast — practice this breakfast before key sessions.
  - **Caffeine:** **3-6 mg/kg, 30-60 min pre-race** is the evidence-backed range (ISSN position
    stand, Guest et al. 2021:
    https://scholarcommons.sc.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1048&context=sph_physical_therapy_facpub);
    response is genotype-dependent and high doses (≥9 mg/kg) add side effects without benefit —
    so it must be rehearsed in training. Avoid caffeine within ~8 h of bedtime.
- **After:** ~1-1.2 g/kg/h carbohydrate in the first 4 h, 8-10 g/kg across the day restores
  glycogen (Cao 2025) — matches the knowledge base's "eat soon after runs."

## 5. What the coach should actually propose (intermediate runner, 3:00-4:00 marathon)

Elite numbers are a ceiling, not a prescription. Racing 6:50-9:10/mi, an intermediate runner has
lower absolute oxidation rates and less gut training time — but also 2-3 hours of depletion risk,
so fueling matters *more* than for elites per kilometer. Recommended coach rules:

**Race-day carbohydrate by goal time (with a trained gut):**

| Goal | Rate | Practical pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Marathon 3:00-3:30 | **75-90 g/h** (build toward 90+) | gel every ~25 min + 6-8% drink at aid stations |
| Marathon 3:30-4:00 | **60-75 g/h** | gel every ~30 min + drink; 45-60 g/h minimum |
| Marathon 4:00+ | **40-60 g/h** | gel every 30-40 min + drink; consistency over quantity |
| Half, sub-2:00 | **30-60 g/h** from the gun | 1-2 gels + drink; fast halves trend toward the high end |
| Half, 2:00+ | optional **15-30 g/h** or nothing | depends on pre-race meal and comfort |

**In-training practice (the coach's structural proposal):**
1. Everyday easy runs: no special fueling (knowledge base unchanged).
2. From the first long run >90 min in the block: start practicing at a modest rate
   (30-45 g/h), split into doses every 20-30 min.
3. Final 6-8 weeks: practice the **goal race rate** during 3-4 of the longest runs and at least
   one midweek marathon-pace session; walk-start the gut build (+5-10 g/h per week back off if
   GI symptoms appear).
4. Rehearse the full race morning (carb-loaded dinner, race breakfast, caffeine timing) before
   the two biggest long runs — race-day simulation, including bottle/gel logistics.
5. If GI trouble persists at 60 g/h after 4+ weeks of practice, cap the rate and re-test; tolerance
   varies widely.

**Hydration & sodium — individualized, not templated:**
- Individualize by sweat rate (weigh before/after a representative 60-75 min run: sweat rate
  ≈ fluid consumed + mass change, per ACSM Position Stand, Sawka et al. 2007:
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17277604). Typical marathon drinking lands around **400-800 ml/h**,
  less in cold, more in heat; avoid both >2% body-mass loss and *overdrinking*.
- **Drink to thirst vs planned drinking:** planned small regular intakes work when thirst lags in
  long hot races; forcing large volumes is the primary cause of exercise-associated hyponatremia
  (International EAH Consensus, Hew-Butler et al.:
  https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2017.00021/full).
- Sodium ~**300-600 mg/h** is the common practical range (salty sweaters / hot races toward the
  high end); sodium in drinks *attenuates* but does not prevent EAH — volume control does.
- Post-race: replace ~150% of fluid deficit with sodium-containing fluids/food over hours.

## 6. Where this updates or contradicts the distilled knowledge base

1. **"~60% of calories from carbohydrate" → switch to g/kg.** Public guidance prescribes absolute
   amounts: ~5-10 g/kg/day in training blocks, **8-12 g/kg/day for the 24-48 h carb load**
   (Kerksick 2017; Cao 2025). Percent-of-calories framing is dated.
2. **"Above roughly the 75-90+ minute mark" — confirmed, with a sharpening.** For *fast* half
   marathons (~<90 min at high intensity), current guidance favors fueling from the start at
   30-60 g/h (60-90 if gut-trained) rather than "nothing needed": the duration threshold interacts
   with intensity (Laura Norris RD summary; SIS half-marathon guide
   https://www.scienceinsport.com/media/pdfs/Half_Marathon_Fuelling_Guide.pdf).
3. **The 90 g/h "ceiling" is no longer a ceiling.** Knowledge-base-era guidance stops at ~90 g/h;
   the 2025-2026 record era normalizes 100-120 g/h for *gut-trained* athletes at elite paces
   (Sawe 115 g/h; Morton 2026 perspective). For the app's typical user: present 60-90 g/h as the
   marathon working range, 100+ as an opt-in extension built gradually.
4. **Onset timing is now explicit.** "Practice gels in long runs" should become "start feeding in
   the first 15-25 min and repeat every 20-30 min" — early onset is a distinct, evidence-backed
   behavior, not just total grams.
5. **No contradiction found** with: "never try anything new on race day" (reinforced by caffeine
   genotype dependence), "everyday easy runs need no special fueling," "never go long stretches
   without eating" (RED-S / IOC 2023: https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/57/17/1073), or the
   recovery/racing-recovery guidance.

## 7. One-paragraph version for the coach prompt (proposed addition)

> Fueling: sessions under ~75-90 minutes at easy intensity need nothing special. For long runs and
> races beyond that, carbohydrate is the limiter: practice taking 30-60 g/h on 60-150-min efforts
> and 60-90 g/h for marathon-intensity work, starting within the first 20 minutes and repeating
> every 20-30 minutes (glucose+fructose sources absorb best). Build tolerance gradually — gut
> training takes weeks, not days. Carb-load 8-12 g/kg/day for 1-2 days before a marathon; eat a
> familiar 1-4 g/kg breakfast 2-4 h before; caffeine (3-6 mg/kg, ~45 min before) only if rehearsed.
> Hydrate to a plan based on your own sweat rate — roughly 400-800 ml/h with 300-600 mg/h sodium —
> and never overdrink. Individualize everything; nothing here overrides medical advice.

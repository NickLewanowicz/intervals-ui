# Public sources for the training knowledge base (80/20 grounding)

**Purpose.** The knowledge base in `packages/core/src/prompts/knowledge.ts` was distilled from two
copyrighted books. Every *principle* it teaches, however, is part of the freely published
exercise-science record. This document maps each knowledge-base section to public, citable,
freely-accessible sources, so the app can (a) ground its coaching advice in public science and
(b) credit Matt Fitzgerald as the *popularizer* of 80/20 without reproducing or promoting his books.

**Reframe in one line:** we do not republish the books; we teach the public science they popularized.

Annotated source list → knowledge-base mapping → suggested app credit statement → nuances where
public science is more cautious than the distilled text.

---

## 1. The academic backbone: Stephen Seiler's intensity-distribution research

| Source | Freely accessible at | What it supports |
|---|---|---|
| Seiler, S. (2010). *What is best practice for training intensity and duration distribution in endurance athletes?* Int J Sports Physiol Perform 5(3):276-291. ~1,265 citations. | Abstract: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20861519 — full-text PDF mirrors are freely linked (e.g. https://umh1617.umh.es/files/2016/05/02-IJSPP_2010_Seiler_0009.pdf) | The foundational review: elite athletes across sports converge on ~80% of training time below the first ventilatory threshold, with the remaining ~20% distributed between threshold and high-intensity work. Basis for the "80/20 finding," the 3-zone physiological definitions, and the "polarized" model. |
| Stöggl, T. & Sperlich, B. (2014). *Polarized training has greater impact on key endurance variables than threshold, high intensity, or high volume training.* Frontiers in Physiology 5:33. | https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2014.00033/full | Controlled trial: polarized distribution improved VO2max, time-to-exhaustion and peak performance (+5.1%) more than threshold-, HIIT-, or volume-focused distributions in trained athletes. |
| Stöggl, T. & Sperlich, B. (2015). *The training intensity distribution among well-trained and elite endurance athletes.* Frontiers in Physiology (open access). | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4621419 | Descriptive review of how elite runners, cyclists, skiers, triathletes and rowers actually distribute intensity — supports "elites independently converged on ~80/20" and the long-run-as-cornerstone observation. |
| Seiler, S. (2022). *Polarized training is optimal for endurance athletes* (position paper, with invited counterpoint). Med Sci Sports Exerc. | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35136001 | The formal pro/con debate — useful to show the app that 80/20 is a dominant-but-debated model, not dogma. |
| Seiler interviews & lectures (free): Scientific Triathlon ep. 177 notes; Fast Talk "Deep dive into 80/20"; Fast Talk Labs polarized-training pathway. | https://scientifictriathlon.com/tts177 · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bxEaAbcO64s · https://www.fasttalklabs.com/pathways/polarized-training | Practitioner-facing explanations of polarized training in Seiler's own words: ~90% of elite training below VT1 in many sports, "make the easy easy and the hard hard," and the point that polarized principles matter most from ~7 h/week upward. |

## 2. Trials in recreational runners (the claim the knowledge base makes directly)

| Source | Freely accessible at | What it supports |
|---|---|---|
| Muñoz, I., Seiler, S., Bautista, J., España, J., Larumbe, E., Esteve-Lanao, J. (2014). *Does polarized training improve performance in recreational runners?* Int J Sports Physiol Perform 9(2):265-272. | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23752040 (full abstract text retrievable via NCBI E-utilities) | The key recreational-runner RCT: 10 weeks, 30 runners, HR-controlled. Polarized (77/3/20) improved 10K by **5.0%** vs **3.6%** for between-thresholds training (46/35/19); the group difference alone was not significant, but the compliant-subset analysis was (Cohen ES 1.29, P=.038). Abstract conclusion: "Polarized training can stimulate greater training effects than between-thresholds training in recreational runners." |
| Rivera-Köfler, T. et al. (2025). *Effects of polarized training vs. other training intensity distribution models…: a scoping review.* J Strength Cond Res 39(3):373-385. | PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39714232 — free PDF: https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Effects-of-Polarized-Training-vs.-Other-Training.pdf | Scoping review: POL and pyramidal models appear most effective in elite/world-class athletes; in recreational athletes differences between intensity distributions are small. Grounds the knowledge base's own caveat that "80/20 is a population optimum, not a precision target." |
| Fitzgerald's own public response to a 2021 challenge study (his official site). | https://www.8020endurance.com/new-study-strikes-fatal-blow-to-80-20-training-philosophy | Shows the 80/20 community engaging publicly with contrary evidence; useful precedent for the app's "evidence-first, non-dogmatic" tone. |

| Source | Freely accessible at | What it supports |
|---|---|---|

## 8. Where public science is more cautious than the distilled knowledge base

1. **Muñoz 2014 precision.** The knowledge base says 80% low-intensity training "produced
   clearly better race improvements" than 50-65% low in controlled trials. The primary abstract
   reports 5.0% vs 3.6% improvement with the *group* difference **not significant**; significance
   appeared only in the compliant-subset analysis (ES 1.29, P=.038). Recommended phrasing:
   "greater improvements (5.0% vs 3.6%), clearly better among runners who adhered to the
   prescribed distribution."
2. **Distribution model ≠ destiny at recreational level.** Rivera-Köfler et al. (2025) conclude
   POL/PYR advantages concentrate in elite athletes, with small differences among recreational
   athletes. The knowledge base already hedges ("population optimum, not a precision target"),
   which matches public science — keep that hedge prominent and avoid implying recreational
   runners must be strictly polarized. The moderate-dose guidance (1 threshold + ≤2 hard
   sessions/week) is consistent with all reviewed models.
3. **"~60% of calories from carbohydrate" is dated.** Public nutrition guidance (ISSN 2017;
   2016 ACSM/AND/DC joint position stand) prescribes carbohydrate in **g/kg/day**
   (5-10 g/kg/d training; 8-12 g/kg/d loading). Update the knowledge base wording — see
   `fueling-2026.md`.
4. **ACWR is contested.** The ~110% rule is a defensible coaching heuristic, but note that
   ACWR's predictive validity is debated (Impellizzeri et al., BJSM). The knowledge base's
   phrasing ("within about 110%... step cycles keep this automatic") is appropriately modest.
5. **Long-run share (20-30% of weekly volume) and cycle lengths (9/12/15/18 weeks)** are
   coaching conventions without direct RCT support; the public record supports their ingredients
   (polarized weekly structure, load-progress conservatism) but not the specific numbers. Frame
   them as heuristics in any user-facing explanation.
